# Sales Playbook

Trades owners buy from people they trust, and they can smell a pitch. Lead with questions, prove the product with a live demo on their own phone, and remove all the risk with a free trial.

## Who to visit

- Owner-operated HVAC and plumbing shops with 2 to 15 people, within your driving radius
- Signs of pain: Google reviews that say "never called back" or "couldn't get through," a voicemail when you call at 2pm, the owner's cell listed as the business number
- Signs they can pay: they run Google Local Services ads or have a directory listing (they already pay $30 to $100+ per lead), and they have trucks with the logo on them

**Best times:** 7 to 8am at the shop before trucks leave, or the supply house counter from 6:30 to 8am. Never in the middle of a summer heat wave or a winter freeze. Come back in the slow season.

## Discovery questions (Phase 1: don't pitch)

1. "Walk me through what happens when the phone rings and you're on a job."
2. "How many calls a week would you guess go to voicemail? What about after 5 and on weekends?"
3. "When someone gets your voicemail, what do you think they do?"
4. "What's a typical service call worth to you? What about an emergency call or a replacement?"
5. "How long does it usually take you to get a quote out? When do you write them?"
6. "Who follows up on quotes that don't close? Do you ask for Google reviews?"
7. "Have you tried an answering service, or thought about hiring office help? What happened?"
8. "If you could hand one thing in your business to someone else tomorrow, what would it be?"

Write down their exact words. "I lose probably three calls a day in July" is a line for your case study and your ads.

## The demo (the strongest pitch)

1. Ask for their phone. Have them call your **demo line** (set up as "Riverside Plumbing & Heating", or better, a shop record already set up with *their* name, which takes 10 minutes in the dashboard before the visit).
2. Let them play a customer: "My water heater is leaking all over the garage."
3. Watch them hear it get the address, offer a real arrival window, and book it.
4. Show the text summary that lands on *your* phone as the "owner." That text is what sells it.
5. Have them try to break it: a gas smell (it gives safety instructions and transfers immediately), a price shopper (it only quotes prices they approve), a salesperson (it politely hangs up).

No signal? Use **Test call in browser** on the shop's dashboard page. It works on a laptop or phone, with a mic or by typing.

## The offer

> "Let's forward just your after-hours calls to it for two weeks, free. Nothing changes during the day. After two weeks I'll sit down with you and show you every call it caught and every job it booked. If it didn't pay for itself, turn it off. No hard feelings."

- The first 10 customers get the $500 setup fee waived in exchange for a testimonial and permission to use their numbers.
- **Pro ($449)** is the default recommendation. **Starter ($249)** is the fallback for a price-sensitive owner who only wants after-hours coverage. **Plus ($799)** is for shops with a lot of open quotes or HVAC maintenance agreements.

## Objections

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "Customers hate robots." | "Call it right now and tell me if you'd hang up. And it's clear with callers that it's your virtual assistant. The alternative is voicemail, and most people won't leave one." |
| "What if it screws up an emergency?" | "Emergencies aren't left to the AI. Gas, CO, sparks and flooding trigger fixed rules: safety instructions, then it transfers straight to your cell. If anything breaks on our end, it transfers to you too." |
| "I already have an answering service." | "What do you pay per month? Do they book into your calendar? Do they know a no-heat call in January is urgent? We're flat-rate, trained on your services, and we text you every call." |
| "Jobber / ServiceTitan is adding AI." | "Great, we'll work alongside it. Their AI is self-serve. I set this up with you, tune it to your shop, and I'm the person you call when something's off." |
| "$449 is a lot." | "What's one service call worth to you? [They answer.] So if it books one extra job a month, it's paid for. The trial shows you the actual number before you pay a cent." |
| "I don't have time to set it up." | "That's my job. Two hours with you, one time, and I do the rest." |
| "Let me think about it." | "Totally fair. How about the trial on after-hours only? You don't have to decide anything for two weeks." |

## Trial close (day 14)

1. Open the shop's dashboard together. Read the numbers out loud: calls answered, after-hours calls, jobs booked, estimated revenue captured.
2. Play one or two transcripts where it caught a job they would have lost.
3. Ask: "Want to keep it on?" Then stop talking.
4. Yes: click **Create payment link**, text it to them, and offer the upgrade to 24/7 answering.
5. No: ask what would have to be true for it to be worth it. Write it down. That's product feedback.

## Referral and partners

- A customer who refers a shop that signs gets one free month.
- Supply houses: offer a lunch-and-learn ("How to stop losing after-hours calls") and leave flyers at the counter.
- Trade-business coaches and equipment distributors: offer a referral fee.
